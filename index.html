Gridded Keetch–Byram Drought Index for Hawaiʻi and the U.S. Pacific Islands

The Keetch–Byram Drought Index (KBDI) estimates the soil-moisture deficit in the upper soil and duff, from 0 (saturated) to 800 (absolutely dry), in hundredths of an inch of water. It is the drought term that fire-danger systems use to judge how much deep fuel is available to burn. These maps compute it every day on a fine grid, using a method similar to the KBDI product the Texas A&M Forest Service produces daily for the State of Texas, with the best daily rain and temperature data available for each island group. Recent history

Against the RAWS

Remote Automatic Weather Stations (RAWS) carry their own KBDI, computed in the Fire Environment Mapping System (FEMS) from the station's measured rain and temperature. Each row compares that with the map cell containing the station. Statistics cover the whole checked record; see Validation.

Reading the scale

The map colours step every 100 units. KBDI is cumulative: it rises a few units on each warm dry day and drops by 100 for every inch of rain after the first 0.20 inch of a wet spell. NWS Red Flag criteria use KBDI ≥ 500 in Hawaiʻi (NWS Honolulu) and KBDI ≥ 600 on Guam (NWS Guam), each together with wind and humidity conditions (see the criteria card above). Tropical islands with high annual rain dry quickly between rains, so values here run higher than mainland experience would suggest; compare a place against its own history, not against another climate.

Built daily from public data: HCDP (University of Hawaiʻi), NOAA MRMS, RTMA and GHCN-Daily, Iowa Environmental Mesonet, PRISM Climate Group, the Rainfall Atlas of Hawaiʻi, and USDA Forest Service FEMS.
