Validation

How each map compares with the KBDI that FEMS computes at Remote Automatic Weather Stations (RAWS), recomputed every day, and how Hawaiʻi's map lines up with where and when fires burned. The design is in Methods §7.

Reading the station tables

  • r: Pearson correlation of the daily map-cell KBDI with FEMS's station KBDI over the checked period.
  • Bias: mean (map − FEMS). MAE: mean absolute difference, in KBDI units.
  • Within 1 class: share of days the map is in the same or an adjacent 100-unit class as FEMS.
  • Hit / False ≥ 400: of FEMS's days at or above 400, the share the map also put there; of FEMS's days below 400, the share the map put at or above.
  • FEMS R / Map R: the mean annual rainfall each index used at that point (inches).
  • Periods start after each domain's one-year spin-up. AMME CS (Saipan) is checked from 2023 on; its FEMS record before then is not usable (Methods §5).

Hawaiʻi: KBDI on fire days

October 2021 – November 2026. KBDI sampled at each fire's cell on its date, against 20,000 random land cells on every day. POD is the share of fire days at or above a KBDI value; "land" is the share of all land-days at or above it; lift is their ratio. 500 is the NWS Honolulu Red Flag KBDI criterion; 600 (NWS Guam's value, and Honolulu's earlier one) is shown for comparison. AUC is the probability that a fire day's KBDI exceeds a random land-day's. Both annual-rain normals are shown; the map uses the Rainfall Atlas. Snapshot of 19 November 2026.

Under either normal most fire days sit below 500 (median fire-day KBDI about 390–480), and in 2026 almost none reached it. In Hawaiʻi, fire is driven more by fine grass fuels, wind and ignitions than by deep drought, so KBDI is context rather than a trigger; HFO's criteria rightly require wind and humidity as well.
